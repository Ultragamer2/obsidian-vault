TL;DR: Big projects finish by repeating: survey where you stand, pick one reasonable next step closer to done, then build a habit-based plan around it. Not by willpower — by looped progress.

1. What he was doing in 2007
Newport followed his GTD intro with a concrete framework for large academic or work projects. Instead of staring at a massive deliverable, he proposed a repeating 3-step cycle: (a) survey current status, (b) pick a new reasonable task that moves closer to the goal, (c) create a new habit-based plan to execute it. The cycle repeats until done.

2. The framework
- Survey: review the project honestly. What has been completed? Where is the gap?
- Select one task: not the whole project — the next single action that brings you measurably closer.
- Habit plan: schedule the work as recurring blocks, not one heroic session. Small consistent steps beat big intentions.

3. Action box
Pick one large project this week. Write one line: current status. Then name exactly one task that moves it closer. Put it on tomorrow's calendar with a time and place.

Coach check
What project are you surveying right now? Name the one next task. I will hold you to 3 days.
